Making an Attention Tester: Part 1

A big part of my work over the last few years has been attention — specifically, how much of it advertising actually gets. I've led studies on this in the Irish market and was included in Professor Karen Nelson-Field's book on the Attention Economy. It's a subject I find genuinely fascinating.

I wanted to see if I could build something that explains it. Not a slide deck or a document — an actual tool you can use.

The idea was straightforward. You upload a video ad. The tool breaks it down frame by frame, second by second, and shows you a graph of the expected attention that video would get — based on real attention data. Alongside that, it plays back a "window of attention" — the video plays, but only for as long as a real viewer would typically watch it on that platform.

The bit I found most interesting to build was the channel toggle. Different platforms have very different attention profiles. What gets watched on YouTube behaves nothing like what gets watched on TikTok or Facebook. So the tool lets you switch between platforms and the graph animates to reflect each one — same video, completely different story depending on where it lives.
