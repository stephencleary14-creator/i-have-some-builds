What's For Dinner?

My wife Caroline has some specific dietary requirements. Gluten free. Low histamine. Vegetarian. No spice, no acid, no tomato. Add a few personal dislikes on top of that and dinner becomes more of a logic puzzle than a pleasure.

I wanted to do something useful about it. So I built her a page. The whole thing took about 20 minutes.

It's a simple dish finder — a curated list of meals I know she can actually eat, drawn from some of my favourite food creators: Gaz Oakley, Derek Sarno, Rainbow Plant Life, LifebyMikeG. All vegetarian, all adapted to her restrictions.

There are three ways to use it.

Surprise me does exactly what it says. Hit the button and get five random dishes from the full list. Good for when you don't know what you want but you're hungry now.

Search by ingredient lets you type something — halloumi, sweet potato, whatever — and it finds every dish that uses it. Useful when something in the fridge needs eating.

Browse by dish type gives you category buttons — Pasta & Grains, Noodles, Curries & Stews, Soups, Comfort Food, Salads. Pick one and get five ideas from that section.

Each dish card shows the ingredients, a gluten free tag where it applies, and which chef it's inspired by. No recipes — just enough to know what's possible and what to look up.

One thing worth showing: the first draft wasn't perfect. Claude's initial list included a few high-histamine ingredients — peanut butter, tofu, nori, walnuts, pine nuts. So we ran an audit. Everything that didn't pass got replaced or removed. This is the kind of thing that's easy to miss on the first pass and quick to fix when you catch it.

It's a small build. But it's one of the more useful things I've made.
