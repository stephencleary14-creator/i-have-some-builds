Character. Incident. Place.

Orlando Wood's work on advertising effectiveness — particularly in Look Out (2023) and his research with System 1 — identifies three elements that make characters work in advertising. Character. Incident. Place. When attention is fleeting, getting all three right is what makes brand advertising something worth watching.

A character alone isn't enough. You need something happening — an incident, a moment, something to make you look. And you need a setting — a place that gives the scene context and warmth. All three together is what makes a character feel alive rather than like a logo with legs.

I've been building toward this.

First I made the character — a Corinthian-style figurine of me, with the SJC crest, number 9, CLEARY on the base. Then I worked on animations — a kick, a tactics board, keepy-uppies, a ball spin, a press conference. Those gave the character incidents. Things to do.

What I hadn't done yet was give it a place.

Today I built a pipeline to fix that. The old workflow used the same start image every time — the figurine on a black background. Every animation opened from that frame. It worked, but there was no sense of location. No world for the character to exist in.

The new approach generates a purpose-built still first — the right scene, the right pose — then animates from that. Brief in, image out, animation out. The office scene below was built this way. A cosy dimly lit room, warm lamp, standing desk, steaming coffee. The figurine turns to close the laptop and reaches for a drink.

Character. Incident. Place. It's a simple framework but it changes how you think about what you're building. I'm not just making animations — I'm building a character with a life.
