Sweating the brand asset

Tesco recently launched a campaign for their Free Fruit & Veg for Schools initiative. It introduces a character called Plumbert the Fruit Giant. It's lovely work — fun, well branded early and often, with a great track. No wonder it scored a 5.9 star rating with System 1.

A character like Plumbert is exactly what Jenni Romaniuk would call a Distinctive Brand Asset in the making. Her work in Building Distinctive Brand Assets is clear on this: assets need to be grown over time. Used consistently. Sweated.

So I went looking for Plumbert beyond the ad. Checked Tesco's website. Nothing. Their Instagram is running cutdowns of the ad, but they're not exactly made for social. For a character with this much personality, that feels like a missed opportunity. Perhaps it's still early days and Tesco has big, giant plans in mind. I know the trade press articles have said the campaign will be live across various channels so it will be great to see how that is done beyond launch.

There is one challenge that Tesco might face. Plumbert is a complicated character to reproduce consistently — and consistency is everything when you're building a DBA.

At The National Lottery a few years ago we introduced a campaign that featured Waterslides. This became a visual DBA, so much so that 94% of Irish adults could recognise The National Lottery when they saw the above image. We used it big, early and often in advertising. We even had them printed on the walls of the office.

It's something I've been thinking about with my own mascot. I've been working hard to keep the face consistent across every still and animation. Same kit, same crest, same base. Even so, I've caught the number 15 appearing on the jersey at times. The model drifts. You have to keep pulling it back.

In a world where AI is increasingly used to generate brand content, this is a real question: if the tool generating your asset can't hold the details, what does that mean for consistency? Plumbert's complexity might make him harder to scale — prompt a model to generate Plumbert and you'll probably get a different fruit giant every time. But that's exactly the kind of problem worth solving if you believe in the asset.

Sweat the asset. Just make sure it stays recognisable while you do. Apples for apples.
