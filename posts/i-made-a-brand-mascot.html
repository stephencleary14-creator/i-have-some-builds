I made a brand mascot

I have spent a lot of time thinking about Distinctive Brand Assets. It is one of the most rigorous and practically useful areas of marketing science. I follow Jenni Romaniuk's work closely — her research through the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute has shaped how I think about what brands actually own in the mind of a buyer.

At the National Lottery we worked on building and measuring DBAs properly. Not just asking "do people like our logo" but tracking distinctiveness, uniqueness, and fame across assets — visual, audio, character. It changes how you think about brand investment. I presented our work at a global research conference in Amsterdam.

So when I started building my personal brand, I wanted to apply the same thinking. One of the asset types I was most interested in was a character — something ownable, memorable, and a bit fun.

Growing up I was obsessed with Corinthian figurines. If you're not familiar — they were small, incredibly detailed football player models. I had dozens. My brother and I then played matches with them.

That format stuck with me. So I asked ChatGPT to create a version of me as one. I gave it a description, a few reference points, and let it work. Then I added some objects around the figurine to give it a bit more personality and context.

Here's the final product.

What I really like is the detail. The CLEARY name carved into the green base. The number 9 and SJC crest on the jersey. And the kit colours match the brand style exactly.

It's now sitting in the header of this blog — and I made a version for the footer too. A small, distinctive, ownable asset. Now to see how flexible I can make the character.
