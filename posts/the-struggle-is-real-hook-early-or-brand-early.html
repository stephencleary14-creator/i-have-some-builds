The Struggle Is Real: Hook early or Brand early?

Disclaimer: this entry might sound negative. Sorry to be a killjoy. I'm generally an optimistic person. In this entry are things I have learned which have helped me create more effective brand work online.

The struggle is real. When it comes to content, there are two things pulling in opposite directions:

  • The need to hook viewers early — grab them before they swipe.
  • The need to be well branded — get the brand seen before they're gone.

"We need to hook the viewer to watch our content."

I agree. People are distracted, swiping, and unfortunately so much content out there isn't watched beyond 2.5 seconds. So we have to do our best to get the viewer to watch as much as possible.

When ads don't meet the Attention-Memory Threshold
of 2.5 seconds, it's hard for memory to grow.

© Amplified Intelligence

"If we hook them they'll watch the content."

To some degree. It's likely a more interesting piece of content will be watched for slightly longer. It's pretty unlikely they'll watch it all. Amplified Intelligence has a theory called Attention Elasticity that I find useful when thinking about this. Each platform has a minimum and maximum level of attention you'd expect to get. A hook would be one way you might reach the upper level of attention on a platform. The size of brand or celebrities used might be another.

"I watched a 5-minute piece of content the other day, why can't we do that?"

When adidas posted their World Cup ad and it clocked in at 5 minutes, I was wondering if other brands would think long-form rules now apply. I think it really depends on which category you operate in. adidas Football is such a popular brand rooted in everyday life, and used celebrities and footballers in the ad. It has all the ingredients for hitting the top levels of attention, and probably smashing it beyond. But brands without that level of category interest should probably stick to the evidence. Most people try to ignore ads as much as possible.

"So should we hook or brand early?"

I'd go for both. Consider a hook that intrigues the viewer while using your distinctive brand assets as a central part of the content. Put the character central to the frame. Blast the brand track. Look at how the logo can be used within context rather than slapped on top of the video.

An example of this in action is a piece of content we made at the National Lottery to celebrate someone winning the EuroMillions Jackpot of €250 Million. It's interesting, well branded in context with the large cheque, and the camera flip at the start is a decent hook. We tested it with Amplified's AI tool and it was a smash hit in terms of expected attention levels.

There are so many constraints. The fun is in making it work within them.
