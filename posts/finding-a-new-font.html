Finding a new font

During my undergraduate degree in Multimedia Design I built websites regularly. One part of that was picking fonts — for logos, headings, body copy. It could take a while. You'd end up on dafont.com or Google Fonts, flicking through options, trying to picture how they'd look in context. Time consuming, a bit of a rabbit hole.

My personal brand was using Inter. It's a good font. Precise, clean, reliable. But the verdict from Claude's assessment was pretty direct: too neutral. Reads as default. The brand has more warmth and personality than Inter conveys.

So I briefed Claude to find me a new one.

What came back wasn't just a list of names — it was a full font explorer page. Seven alternatives, each rendered at multiple weights and sizes in my actual wordmark, across all three brand backgrounds: light, cream, and dark. Character notes. A verdict on how the red accent j sits in each one. All the information needed to make the call.

The alphabet comparison view was the most useful. It shows every font rendering the same string — "stephenjcleary" — so you can see exactly how each one handles the letters that actually matter in your name.

Outfit was the one. Geometric with intentional warmth — 45° terminals and rounded joints make it feel friendly without being casual. The j in particular sits nicely in crimson: clean dot, rounded curve at the base. It feels part of the wordmark, not just a letter. The verdict: a strong middle ground — modern and approachable. Distinctive at large sizes.

Once the decision was made, the style guide updated to Outfit across the board.
