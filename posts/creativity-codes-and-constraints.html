Creativity, Codes & Constraints

A few years ago I worked on an ad for The National Lottery. It was built around a brilliant organisation called Lough Ree Access for All, a group who built an accessible boat so that everyone, regardless of ability, could get out on the water and experience the lough.

When you're making an ad for a charity or a third-party cause, the emotional angles are everywhere. The people who built it. The service users. The challenges that were overcome. The volunteers. The challenge isn't finding the emotion. It's deciding which emotion to follow, and how to tell a complete story in 40 seconds while making sure the brand still gets noticed.

I've been listening to Adam Morgan's podcast Let's Make This More Interesting recently. A conversation with Magda Osinska from Aardman stuck with me. She talked about the challenge and the thrill of working within constraints: the stop-motion process, the tiny sets, the physical limitations of the craft. But they make it work. And they make it work beautifully. Maybe it's the constraints that keep the work focused to complete the job it's meant to do.

The Lough Ree ad was packed with the emotion of a real tightly knit community, but carefully crafted. We had a charming voiceover from a key member of the team. We selected the frames with the most joy from service users out on the water. It was also well-branded, because it needed to be. It was all wrapped up and presented clearly by the National Lottery. The logo early, the key funding message at the end, and the brand track running throughout.

We tested it with System 1 at the time. It scored 5.6 stars. That's genuinely rare. Around 1% of ads ever reach that level. The emotional intensity was rated exceptional. Brand fluency hit 92%. Participants felt pleased for others. Uplifted.

A key character. An unfolding story. A clear sense of place. Well-branded.

Creativity and codes, co-existing.
